# Writing books

The style comes from the book's theme, reader and genre, decided before the first sentence and kept to the last. Experimental skill.

## 1. Brief

Fix these before writing. Take them from the request; infer the rest and state the inference.

- **Reader:** age and what they want (thrill, comfort, laughter, understanding).
- **Genre and theme.** If the request gives a theme but no genre ("a family with a vineyard in the 1960s"), offer two or three readings with one line each (e.g. family saga, historical crime, memoir), pick one, and say which.
- **Language and orthography:** pt-PT post-1990 agreement unless asked otherwise. Never mix "tecto" and "teto" in one text. pt-PT vocabulary, not pt-BR ("discussões", not "brigas").
- **Length:** opening, chapter, short story or full book.

## 2. Style sheet

Write it in 5–8 lines before the text and keep it for the whole book: POV and distance, tense, sentence rhythm, register, description density, dialogue share, and two moves this book never makes.

Genre defaults (override when the theme asks for it):

| Genre | Defaults |
|-------|----------|
| Thriller | Close third, past; short sentences; chapters end mid-action; information withheld, never explained |
| Literary / family saga | First person retrospective or close third; long and short sentences mixed; sensory detail of place and time |
| Historical | Period objects and words, no modern idiom; the era shown through daily life, not exposition |
| Crime | Clues fair to the reader; the detective's voice carries the tone |
| Romance | Two clear wants in conflict; dialogue carries the tension |
| Fantasy / sci-fi | One rule of the world at a time, shown in use; no glossary dumps |
| Humour / satire | Narrator's irony; precise absurd detail; never laughs at its own joke |
| Children 4–6 | Read-aloud rhythm, repetition, sound words, one feeling per page, no stated moral |
| Middle grade / YA | Protagonist's age is the narrator's age; fast scenes; real stakes |
| Memoir | Honest first person; the older narrator's view only where it adds meaning |

**In the style of an author:** list four or five traits first (narrator stance, sentence shape, vocabulary, humour, typical targets) and write from the traits. For an author still under copyright, imitate the traits only. Never reproduce sentences from any author.

## 3. Book-length work

For more than one chapter, keep two files next to the text:
- `style.md`: the style sheet.
- `bible.md`: characters (name, age, look, voice), places, timeline, facts already stated.

Read both before each chapter. Update `bible.md` after it.

## 4. Revision pass

Reread the text and remove these AI habits. Each one appears in untested drafts:
- **Closing aphorism:** a final line that sums up the theme ("até os sorrisos se fabricavam"). End on an image or an action.
- **Explained simile:** "como quem guarda vinho velho" after the meaning is already clear. Keep one per page at most.
- **Hindsight foreshadowing:** "só mais tarde percebi", "seria a última vez". Use it once per book at most.
- **Tense drift:** "batiam… tamborilam". One tense per scene.
- **Orthography or variant mix:** pt-PT and pt-BR, pre- and post-1990 spelling.
- **Stated emotion:** "sentiu medo". Show the body or the action instead.

## Output

1. The brief (with the chosen reading if the genre was open) and the style sheet.
2. The text.
3. One line per revision-pass fix you made, at most five.
